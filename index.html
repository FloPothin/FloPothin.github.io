About

My dissertation, The Decarbonization Gap: Firms, Nations, and the Economics of the Low-Carbon Transition, asks: why do the climate commitments of firms and countries so often change what is reported more than what is emitted?

I study what economic agents say they do about climate change and what they actually do. I show that disclosure choices and the reallocation of production through trade move emissions on paper and across borders more than they reduce them, while a carbon price does cut them, by an amount its allocation rule decides.

I prepared my doctorate under an industrial research agreement with Toovalu, a company that develops carbon-accounting software, and I continue to work with the company on applied research: artificial intelligence and quantitative methods that help companies decarbonize, and methods to measure the environmental impact of AI systems themselves.

I am a member of the Science Based Targets initiative's expert working group on data quality, assurance and claims, and of IRIS-E, the Université de Rennes initiative on the socio-ecological transition. I teach macroeconometrics and macrodynamics at master's level in Rennes.

I am originally from Le Tampon, on Réunion Island.
